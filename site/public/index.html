Dictation for macOS that runs on your Mac and learns how you talk.

Hold Fn, talk, let go. The text lands in whatever field you were in: fillers gone, false starts fixed, "actually, make it Tuesday" applied. Speech-to-text never leaves your machine. No account.

Names and terms are spelled right because you corrected them once. FnSpeak picks vocabulary up from your projects folder, contacts and calendar, and learns from what you retype.

Local by default

Whisper or Parakeet on your Mac. On most Apple Silicon machines the cleanup model runs locally too. If it's slow, missing, or makes something up, you get the rule-cleaned text instead. You never lose a dictation.

Reads the room

It looks at the field you're typing into. Slack gets casual, Mail gets prose, the terminal gets exactly what you said. "Scratch that" drops the last sentence. "Send it" presses Return if you turn that on.

Notes and meetings

⌥Space opens a quick note bar. Meeting mode records mic and call audio, transcribes as you go, and writes a summary with decisions and action items. Audio isn't kept. A bundled MCP server lets your AI tools search your notes, read-only, local.

How it works

  1. Speech to textwhisper.cpp or sherpa-onnx Parakeet, with your vocabulary passed in as a prompt.
  2. Rule filterStrips um, uh, I-I, the the. Instant, and the fallback if anything downstream fails.
  3. Vocabulary matchMisheard names matched by sound against your words: PG vector → pgvector.
  4. Cleanup modelA small language model, run through llama.cpp as a local sidecar, handles like, you know, restarts, and corrections that reach back a few sentences.
  5. GuardrailRejects any rewrite that adds words you didn't say.

Cloud mode, if you choose it, sends text only, never audio. Dictations with no self-corrections go to a cheaper model.

Lots of apps do this. Here's what's different.

Local Whisper, a cleanup model, per-app tone, learning from corrections: VoiceInk, Handy, OpenWhispr, Superwhisper and Wispr Flow all do some of it, and a few do it well. These are the parts we couldn't find anywhere else.

  • It already knows your words before you dictate.Names and terms are pre-seeded from your projects folder, Contacts and Calendar, then refined by what you retype. Other apps start from a dictionary you fill in by hand, or read what's on screen at that moment.
  • It can't put words in your mouth.A guardrail rejects any rewrite that adds something you didn't say. If the model is slow, missing or wrong, you get the rule-cleaned transcript instead, and there's an undo on the pill. We didn't find another app with an output check like this.
  • The cleanup model is open, not a blob.The training scripts, data recipe and results are in the repo under MIT. Elsewhere the "refine" step is a closed runtime inside an otherwise open app.
  • There's a benchmark you can run on anyone.fnspeak-bench scores word error rate, vocabulary spelling, fillers left behind and self-corrections applied, on your own clips. The unflattering numbers are on this page.
  • Your AI tools can read your notes, read-only, offline.The MCP server in the app bundle makes no network calls and can't write unless you ask it to.

What they have that we don't, yet: a notarized download, Windows and Linux, speaker names in meeting notes, snippets, and voice-editing selected text. Compared against the apps' own docs and READMEs in November 2026.

Is it any good?

fnspeak-bench scores the pipeline on word error rate, whether your vocabulary came out spelled right, fillers left behind, and whether self-corrections were applied. The numbers below are from the first training run of a small cleanup model, including the ones that aren't flattering. Run it on your own voice; the clips and manifests are in the repo.

Short version: the 4B baseline gets 1.1% word error rate on the 11 TTS clips. The 1.7B model I fine-tuned gets 2.2% at about half the latency (167 ms against 301 ms), and on the 200 held-out validation pairs it beats the 4B, which falls back to the rule filter 13 times. The validation set follows the training data's conventions, so take that second number with salt.

Sync, teams and self-hosting

Everything above works with no account. If you want your notes on two Macs, a shared glossary with your team, or cleanup without running a model or managing a key, the app can sign in to a server. The server is in the repo: one binary, SQLite or Postgres, docker compose in deploy/. Run your own or use the hosted one. Either way audio never leaves your Mac; the server only sees text you choose to sync.
